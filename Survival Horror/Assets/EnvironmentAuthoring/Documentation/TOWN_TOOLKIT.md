# Roads, sidewalks, walls and fences

These are individual objects added to your existing Unity level. They do not create a new level or a House. The internal code prefix Town refers to these tools together, not to another object you must create.

## Basic roads and sidewalks

Choose **Road** or **Sidewalk** in the persistent **Environment Tools** Scene palette (or **GameObject > Environment**). Click a surface or the ground plane to create the object and its first drawing point; no object is created until that click. Continue clicking to chain straight segments; Escape or right-click ends the chain. Width outlines show the actual proposed surface. Existing layouts open in Select mode.

Roads generate a continuous closed surface from the path, with bevelled bends and shared intersections. No road module, turn asset or junction list is required. Set road width, thickness, material and texture repeat distance. Left sidewalk / Right sidewalk add a raised strip with its own width, sidewalk height and material. Sides follow each segment's drawing direction. The whole road footprint is removed from the sidewalk footprint, so sidewalks do not cap road ends or cross intersection mouths.

A standalone sidewalk uses the same footprint construction, with width, thickness, material and repeat distance. Path points have independent XYZ positions. Drawing raycasts scene surfaces, ignoring the object's own generated children, and falls back to the object's horizontal plane when there is no hit. Road and sidewalk heights interpolate along the path; curbs follow the same elevations. Settle down casts downward for selected points, or all points when none are selected, and leaves misses unchanged. This places points rather than automatically draping the entire surface over terrain; add points where the slope needs to change. Curves, banking and overpasses inside one path object are unsupported. Use separate objects for vertically overlapping routes.

Click points, lines or visible generated surfaces to select. Shift adds and Ctrl removes; drag empty space to box-select points. Move selected points or segments directly, or use the XYZ Move gizmo (W). The gizmo follows Unity's Global/Local orientation. Shift bypasses the grid while retaining source-point/line snapping. Delete removes selected segments, or a selected point and its incident segments. Dissolve joins two compatible incident segments; higher-degree junctions remain explicit. Delete with no logical selection cannot delete the root while editing.

Only the accepted release publishes a drag. Escape, navigation and capture loss cancel it. The path and settings are authored Undo data; generated geometry publishes in the same Undo action. External asset/script changes show a stale notice and wait for Rebuild; background and Undo callbacks never replace native children. Save your scene normally. Reopening retains path identities, materials, render meshes and collision. The generators do no runtime rebuilding.

Materials are object-wide for roads and sidewalks. The supplied defaults are Asphalt.mat for roads and Cobblestone.mat for standalone/road sidewalks, under Assets/EnvironmentAuthoring/Content/TownKits/Basic. Colour, normal and packed roughness maps use URP Lit; displacement is unused. Repeat (m) is the distance covered by one tile: lower values repeat more. Material Tiling changes the finish independently (useful for road sidewalks, which share the road repeat setting).

Surface UVs follow the nearest span axis per face: U along the path and V across, in local metres divided by Repeat. Straight collinear sections keep texture phase, including reversed drawing directions. Bends/intersections can have simple UV seams. Vertical sides/caps retain extrusion mapping. Texture alignment adds no polygons. The supplied paving defaults use these UVs. Optional construction materials have their own projection; they do not follow path direction. There is no mandatory texture atlas. Per-segment model/front/back overrides belong to wall/fence runs, not to road surfaces.

## Optional curbs

Curbs is available on both sidewalks and roads. It adds a fixed 12 cm border inside the final joined sidewalk footprint. Curb height (m) sets its rise above the sidewalk surface, independently of sidewalk height/thickness: 8 cm by default, minimum 1 cm. Curbs have their own generated mesh, renderer and matching collision mesh. Curb material override is optional: clear it to inherit the sidewalk finish. Turning Curbs off retains the height and material override. Road intersection mouths remain open and internal span joins do not receive curb seams. The footprint/elevation/collision owner is shared with the sidewalk, not another path network.

## Walls and fences

Choose **Wall** in the Environment Tools palette; select a fence family in its properties. These retain shared authored panels, optional upright posts, per-segment model/material overrides, actual XYZ points and surface-raycast drawing. Settle down casts downward for selected points, or all points when none are selected. Panels step horizontally on slopes; they are not bent to the terrain. Roads/sidewalks instead form a low-poly sloping surface between their points.

Wall panel sources use local X from 0 to Length, Y=0 at their base and Z for thickness. Author closed ends. Material slot 0 is front and slot 1 is back. Create a reusable module through Assets > Create > Environment > Town > Module. Old road-template meshes may still be used as ordinary manual level assets; the road generator no longer reads road connection/footprint metadata or matches modular junctions.

## Downloaded fence families

Assets/EnvironmentAuthoring/Content/FenceKits/Downloaded contains seven FenceFamily catalogs (Families/<style>.asset), 39 native repeat-panel modules, 18 source posts plus one extracted metal upright, and reusable prefabs for all 79 source pieces. Gates, corners and partial/broken specials are manual prefabs rather than automatic repeat panels. FenceSources.json records every disposition and original source hash. The existing modular reference scene, Assets/EnvironmentAuthoring/Reference/WallModules/WallModuleDemo.unity, displays the full inventory and seven editable example runs.

In the existing fence properties panel, choose Fence family, then Panel and Post. Custom fields remain available. Family selection copies explicit asset/default dimensions into the run; it is not a live lock. Front/back and per-span overrides remain independent. Posts controls every endpoint, shared path node and internal panel seam; off/on retains the selected mesh, native material slots, width and height. Post material override applies one finish uniformly; clear it to restore authored slots (or the run's front override). Zero post height matches the run height; a positive height keeps the authored post height. Width scales both cross-section axes uniformly.

Imported detailed panels set AllowStretch=false: native width/height is retained, with whole repeats and a UV-preserving residual crop through the existing House crop owner. Cropping may remove a central rail/picket/detail, an intentional low-poly approximation; choose a suitable variant or adjust the run if the cut is conspicuous. Plain custom modules can allow shared-mesh transform fitting; authored UVs then stretch with that geometry. Panels step horizontally at each panel's midpoint elevation on slopes, while posts sit on the path. Very steep or short cropped runs can expose this approximation; no terrain draping or profile solver is added.

To author a new family, create Town Module assets with closed repeat meshes (X 0..Length, base Y=0, centred depth Z), correct native dimensions/material slots and AllowStretch policy. Create Assets > Create > Environment > Fence Family and assign Panels plus Post mesh/material entries. This is a small explicit catalog, not a filename parser. Development-only downloaded-pack import recipes are not installed.

## Workflow and responsibilities

Keep generator roots upright at unit scale; translation and yaw rotation are supported. Unpack prefab instances before changing their generated layout. Edit source paths/settings rather than generated children. Unknown substituted output meshes are retained hidden for deliberate recovery rather than overwritten.

TownPath owns points, straight segments and stable identities. TownAuthoring owns scalar/path validity. TownCommands publishes one authored edit with Undo. TownEditingContext owns mode, hover, selection and one active gesture; ScenePointerInput supplies the native navigation/control adapter. TownPathGeometry owns footprint union and extrusion; the small road and sidewalk builders assign those surfaces to the existing output owner. TownRebuild reconciles generated parts and shared mesh resources. Runtime has no editor-UI dependency. One continuous straight-run elevation sampler raises surface meshes, collision and previews. Non-flat surfaces use an indexed planar mesh with shared top/bottom/cap edges and retain authored height stations without automatic length-based subdivision. Long spans stay low-poly; add points for more slope detail. Junction transitions can be faceted. Sub-millimetre clipping debris is removed before triangulation; extremely thin tips can be blunted. There is no spline package, road-port solver or automatic terrain deformation.

The example at Assets/EnvironmentAuthoring/Reference/TownToolkit/TownToolkit.unity is demonstration content. Use your own scene for production. Verification and remaining limitations are recorded in RESTART_CHECKPOINT.md.

Material organization: see [the material library](MATERIAL_LIBRARY.md). Shared surfaces and path-UV alternatives live under Assets/EnvironmentAuthoring/Content/Materials/Surfaces; baked fence finishes live under ModelSpecific/Fences. Fence FBX source slots remap to those shared assets, so browsing the imported models does not add per-model material copies.
