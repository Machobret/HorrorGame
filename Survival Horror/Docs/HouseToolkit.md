# House Toolkit

## Wires

Choose **Environment Tools > Wire** or **GameObject > Environment > Wire**. Click surfaces to place nodes. The second click creates the wire. Right-click to end the run, then click any existing node to start another. Right-drag navigates the Scene view. Enter switches to node editing. Drag a node onto another surface or use its XYZ gizmo. Double-click a segment to insert a node. Delete removes the selected node while at least two remain. Select an existing wire and choose **Edit Wire** in **Procedural Env Properties** to resume. Select any node and choose **Draw** to branch from it. Click another existing node to connect the run. Junctions are protected from node deletion. Set **Thickness (mm)** in the Inspector or Procedural Env Properties. Each strand defaults to 18 mm. **Number of wires** sets the bundle size for the entire object (1?8). Select a segment to adjust **Segment sag (%)**. This sets its midpoint drop relative to its horizontal span. Existing segments start straight.

## Street litter

Open **Environment Tools > Scatter** or **GameObject > Environment > Scatter**. Choose **Paper Trail**, **Loading Bay**, or **Gutter Litter** for paper and packaging. The brush opens in **Procedural Env Properties**. Click a thumbnail to toggle that variant for painting and erasing. Checked thumbnails participate in the preset?s weighted mix. Double-click to reveal its source mesh. **Gutter Debris** mixes dry leaves, twigs, and occasional cigarette filters. **Loading Bay Hardware** mixes packing straps, wood splinters, and pallet blocks. **Broken Masonry** mixes plaster flakes, brick chips, and concrete fragments. Drag on the street to paint. Radius sets the brush footprint and density sets the expected number of pieces per square metre in a dab. Use **Flat** or **Solid** to restrict painting and erasing. Hold Shift or select **Erase** to remove pieces of the current preset. Escape returns to selection. Unity Undo restores an entire stroke.

Shared preset assets live in `Assets/Game/Environment/StreetLitter`. Each group has a relative weight, a material, and one or more single-submesh, readable mesh variants. Each placement stores its chosen mesh, material, and pose. Editing a preset's weights affects future strokes. Street Scatter groups the results in 12-metre cells with one combined mesh per occupied cell and a submesh per material. Placements stay at their painted scene positions when a road is rebuilt.

## Warehouse markings

Choose **Environment Tools → Marking** (also **GameObject → Environment → Marking**), then draw a path on the floor. In **Procedural Env Properties → Marking**, set **Width (m)**, **Color**, **Wear**, and **Wear scale**. Wear runs from 0 (fresh) through 1 (heavily chipped) to 2 (fully worn away); above 1, connected scuffs erode the remaining paint into smaller patches. Wear scale defaults to 1; larger values make larger worn patches and smaller values make finer chips. Both use per-instance shader data and retain the shared material. **Reseed wear** changes the chips without making a new material. **Selection** edits point locations, including Y. Points and whole segments have XYZ gizmos; Shift constrains horizontal movement, Alt disables snapping, double-click inserts a point, Delete dissolves a two-segment point, and F frames the selection. Vertical gizmo movement preserves the point's off-grid X/Z position.

**Tool Properties → Attach to surface** fits drawing to supporting floor/ramp colliders. Toolkit walls, doors, frames and fixtures do not divert the paint. Paint can continue beneath walls even when adjacent room slabs stop at the wall boundary. Across genuine gaps, supported stretches are kept and only unsupported portions are omitted, together in one Undo; the drawing chain continues. The preview shows supported stretches in green and gaps in red. Add points at slope changes. Explicit Y movement remains manual. **Refit to surface** refits existing points and clips unsupported stretches while preserving supported paint. Surface fitting samples every 5 cm with refined transition boundaries; smaller holes are outside its resolution.

Markings automatically live under **Markings**. Every strip and bounded corner uses the same quad mesh and shared instanced paint material, with tint, wear, seed and corner positions passed per instance. They create no gameplay colliders and perform no runtime surface queries. The warehouse review scene is `Assets/Sandbox/Markings/WarehouseMarkings.unity`; recreate it with **Tools → Procedural Environment → Create Marking Example**. It demonstrates fresh/default/heavily worn paint, angular corners, and a ramp transitioning onto a landing.

## Wall decals

Open **Window > Game > Asset Library > Decals** and drag a swatch into the Scene. The drop sets the initial surface orientation. Each poster is an independent mesh under **Decals** with ordinary Move, Rotate, and Scale handles. It can span openings, corners, and multiple buildings. Select it and choose a family and replacement swatch in the Inspector to change its artwork. Older wall-owned posters convert to scene objects when their scene opens.

The Stackspace and Workshop and Street poster atlases live in `Assets/Game/Environment/WallDecals`. The source sheets, stable poster IDs, themes, and repack script are in `ArtSource/Environment/WallDecals`. To author another atlas, create a **Procedural Environment → Wall Decal Atlas** asset with a shared material and one entry per poster. Give each entry a stable ID, texture rectangle, size in metres, and one or more themes. Add padding around its texture region for filtering and mipmaps.

Choose **GameObject → Environment → House** or **Road** for another network. Open **Window → House Toolkit → Procedural Env Properties**, or use the button of the same name in either tool palette. Select a road network and assign **Town Street**. The **Road** tab edits shared family defaults, including road and sidewalk width, lane counts, curbs, surfaces and markings. Choose **Edit Roads** and click a road part to edit its local overrides in **Selection**. **Tool Properties** controls snapping and the family used when drawing. The embedded package under `Packages/com.manta.house-toolkit` contains the tools and runs independently of the separate toolkit project.

In the Scene view’s **Environment Tools** palette, choose **House**, **Wall**, **Road**, **Sidewalk**, **Ground Area**, or **Filler Building**, then click to place the first point. Esc cancels placement. The same creation tools are under **GameObject → Environment**. Sidewalks and ground areas are independent scene objects. **Draw Sidewalk** draws a centreline. The run inherits its sidewalk object’s width and material. Click a segment in **Select** to edit the whole run or override just that segment. **Ground Area** draws flat pavement on its object’s local plane. Close a loop to fill it, connect two points on a boundary to split it, or draw back to an existing edge to append a face. Click inside to edit the selected face material. Click an edge to move its corners. For pavement already drawn inside a road network, **Road → Separate existing pavement** moves its paths and faces to independent objects in one Undo. Both tools use the local grid, Shift for 45-degree alignment, Alt-click for free placement, and Esc to end a chain.

New environment objects appear under scene groups such as **Buildings**, **Roads** and **Shelves**. When a level with generated environment content is saved, loose environment building and vehicle prefabs also join **Buildings** and **Vehicles**. Storage and furniture prefabs go into **Props**. Manually nested objects keep their parents. The shared first-level editing scene is `Assets/Game/Levels/Chapter0/Scenes/1stLevel.unity`.

Use **Pin** beside **Cutaway** to keep a house's current cutaway setting on its remembered floor after **Done**. Select that house to toggle or unpin its cutaway outside edit mode. Pins apply to the current editor preview and clear when the scene closes.

To line a street with lamps, choose **Edit Roads**, click the asphalt, then enable **Selection → Repeating streetlights**. **Side** follows the street’s drawing direction. **Both** places a facing pair at each position. **Alternating** starts on the right and switches sides after each placed lamp. **Period (m)** sets spacing along the street and **Offset (m)** slides the pattern from its start. Lamps skip junctions. Shift-select streets to edit them together. In the streetlight prefab, select **Diffuser light → Volumetric cone**. Its local Z scale sets beam length in metres and X/Y set its two radii. The beam billboards around its vertical axis. Vertex alpha softens its sides. The shared **Streetlight Beam** material defaults to fading at 5 m from its envelope and disappearing at 1.5 m.

The gameplay renderer uses Forward+ so streetlights can illuminate large road networks and imported buildings beyond the Forward per-object light limit. This project customization is in `Assets/AssetStore/HorrorEngine/Rendering/HEUniversalPipelineAsset_Renderer.asset`. Realtime shadow distance is 25 m. Additional-light Low and Medium tiers use 128 pixels per shadow face, and High uses 256. The additional-light atlas is 2048 square. The tablet map uses its separate Forward renderer. Retro Composite and Stylized Flare Lighting remain enabled on the gameplay renderer.

Choose **Fixture** while editing a house or standalone wall, pick a swatch in **Tool Properties**, and click the mounting surface. Ceiling placement works in cutaway. For a filler façade, use **Building → Place wall fixture** and click its exterior face. Wall fixtures follow their host through edits and splits. Filler attachments retain their distance along the edge and their height above the storey.

Drag a registered lamp from **Window → Game → Asset Library** into the Scene to attach it to a compatible surface or place it freely. A Hierarchy drop creates a free fixture. Select the fixture root to change **On**, **Color**, **Intensity**, **Range**, **Light offset**, or a spotlight’s **Beam angle**. Edits apply to that instance. Use **Inherit** to restore a setting. **Edit shared defaults** changes the palette entry for that prefab. The diffuser follows the light color and power. The Scene view arrow shows the offset direction even at zero. Mounted lights move outward from the mounting surface. Free lights use the prefab entry’s local **Free Offset Axis**.

To register another prefab, edit **Building Fixtures** under `Assets/Game/Environment/Lighting`. Select its mount and model rotation, assign its masked **Diffuser Material**, and put the Light component on a separate child so offset edits leave the housing fixed. Wall fixtures face fixture-local +Z with their top toward +Y.

Choose **Environment Tools → Shelf**, click the first endpoint, and click again to place a bay run. Continue around a corner or press Esc to finish. The preview shows the fitted bays. Double-click a rack in **Select** to insert a path point. The default family is 2 m tall and 0.6 m deep, with four timber decks. In **Procedural Env Properties**, set **Height (m)**, **Depth (m)** and **Decks** for the selected shelf. The reset buttons restore the family values. **Fill (%)** stocks that fraction of usable box positions per straight run, rounded to the nearest slot. Raising it fills gaps while keeping existing boxes in place. Boxes fit the clearance under the deck above. **Seed** changes the arrangement and box designs. The sandbox scene `Assets/Sandbox/Shelving/StockedShelves.unity` contains straight and L-shaped examples.

Shelf families take one end-frame mesh and one deck mesh. Author both in metres with the bay length along X and depth along Z. Centre the frame feet and the deck's top surface at their respective origins. The frame and deck each use one shared UV material. The sample timber maps long faces and cut ends into the existing worn-wood trim sheet. The optional decorative stock prefab uses a bottom-centred unit footprint. One straight bay is the authored deck length plus the frame thickness. Bays can fit between 78% and 115% of that pitch. At turns the earlier run keeps its full end frame.

In either editor, Shift-click adds or removes the actual surface, insert, or point you clicked. Edit matching items together in **Selection**. For a mixed selection, **Keep only** and **Discard** filter the selection by type. For openings on the same wall, **Move along wall** offsets all of them together and preserves their spacing.

In **Floors**, turn off **Generate ceiling** for an open rooftop. Select a room floor and set **Ceiling** to **Inherit**, **On**, or **Off**. An occupied storey above still owns its structural floor. Choose **NoTrim** in the floor or wall trim override to suppress an inherited baseboard. Clearing the override restores inheritance.

The plain family generates rectangular openings. Storefront uses 2 m, 1 m and 0.5 m panels, a 0.9 × 2.1 m door surround and a 1 × 1 m window surround. Even Spacing is its default. Assign the glass door or another compatible insert in either family. In the house properties, choose interior and exterior finishes independently. Find building finishes in `Assets/Game/Environment/ProjectedFinishes`. Click **Swatches** beside a finish field to browse finishes grouped by surface. Open **All surfaces** to filter by Brick, Concrete, Metal, Paving, Plaster, Tiles or Wood. Select a texture to apply it or **Inherit** to clear the override. Speckled Ivory Tile and its Triplanar variant share one texture and use different mapping shaders. To offer a new finish in the picker, put a material using **House Toolkit/Projected Finish** in its surface folder and add it to **WallFinishes.asset**.

Chapter 1’s APT and electricity puzzle scenes, and the recovery copy, retain their generated meshes, colliders and gameplay objects as static scene content. The material shader in `Assets/Game/Rendering/HorizontalWall.shader` still supplies authored UVs and projection for existing art. The baked wall component restores its saved vertical projection when a scene opens.

Choose **Environment Tools → Wall** and click to start a freestanding wall. In **Procedural Env Properties → Wall**, choose **Metal Fence** for chain-link panels with steel posts and modeled barbs. Closing a freestanding wall loop leaves the ground open.

In **Door** or **Window**, enable **Empty opening** in **Tool Properties** to place the cutout with its authored surround. Click the empty space or frame and use **Selection ? Empty opening** to restore the insert. The opening keeps its dimensions and position while empty. Families can also list a door or window insert asset with just its opening dimensions for a permanently empty option.

The **Door** palette starts with the 0.9 × 2.1 m pedestrian gate, which also fits Plain and Storefront. Its leaf pivots at the hinge while the prefab root centres the doorway. Use **Panel** for the bare **Large Gate Frame**, **Large Gate Closed**, or **Large Gate Sliding**. Each reserves a 5.36 m fence segment. **Large Gate Sliding** uses the same posts and leaf. Interact at the fixed post to open or close it, and leave 5.4 m clear along the track on its opening side. To offer a custom panel on a house or freestanding wall, create a **House Toolkit ? Insert** asset, choose **Panel**, and add it to the wall family's **Family inserts** list. A prefab alone uses its specified width and height. An assigned **Panel Segment** reserves the frame's authored width and positions an optional prefab at the opening. A solid segment can use a zero-size aperture. The surrounding fence panels adjust to the available length. The editable source is `ArtSource/Environment/MetalFence/MetalFence.blend`.

The filler **Building type** dropdown includes Industrial Depot and Industrial Machine Shop. The shop has shorter loading bays and brick upper storeys. Both use the square depot texture for the loading floor.
